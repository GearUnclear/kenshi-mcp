# Making money

Practical ways to earn cats in Kenshi, ordered from what one weak character can do on day one to what needs a trained, equipped squad, with prices, yields, places and risks.

## Cats (c.)
Also: currency, cash, coins, c., what are cats
- Cats are the game's currency. Amounts are written with a "c." prefix: c.1,000 is one thousand cats.
- Your whole faction shares one total, shown as "Money" in the HUD's time and money panel ("Clock and money" in Interface); it is not an item in anyone's inventory.
- Cats found as loot (Cats, String of Cats) join the total when picked up, or when right-clicked in your inventory.
- What things sell for: "Money basics: what sells for how much". Ways to earn: "Earning methods in order of readiness".

## Money basics: what sells for how much
Also: sell price, sell value, value, half price, quarter price, what things sell for
- Money is called Cats and is written "c." on screen (c.1,000).
- Trade goods, materials and food-type goods: traders buy and sell at the same local price. Most non-equipment items are in this group.
- Weapons, armour, crossbows and blueprints: the tooltip shows Value and Sell value; Sell value is 25% of Value (also for gear you crafted).
- Other non-trade items (food, backpacks, maps, robot limbs) sell for 50%.
- Anything flagged stolen sells for half its normal value.
- Shinobi Thieves traders and Thief Fences buy and sell everything at 50% of the local price.
- Each town randomises trade-good prices within plus or minus 30% of base value.

## Earning methods in order of readiness
Also: how to make money, best money method, early money, first income, progression
- Day one, one weak character: mine Copper beside a town and sell it.
- Day one, no fighting: loot bandits and animals that town guards or patrols have knocked out.
- First days: carry downed bandits with small bounties to a police cage; sell captives at a Slave Shop.
- A few thousand cats: trade goods between distant towns; add a backpack, then a pack animal.
- Owning a shack plus research: storage-box mining, then a Clothing Bench making Bandanas.
- Trained thief: steal from shops at night, sell to another faction.
- Squad that can travel: Hashish and alcohol routes, ruin looting, Beak Thing Eggs.
- Squad that can fight: named bounties (c.10,000 to c.100,000).
- The wiki rates mining below almost every other method for income, but it is the only one with no risk beyond wandering bandits.

## Selling items to a trader
Also: how to sell, sell loot, shopkeeper, vendor, barman
- Hold the right mouse button on the shopkeeper or barman, move onto Trade in the menu and release (the menu closes when the button is released). A plain right-click talks to them; shopkeepers also offer trade in dialogue.
- The trade window shows your inventory and the trader's side by side. Drag an item across, or right-click it to move one unit; Shift + right-click moves the whole stack.
- Any shop buys any legal item: ore sells at a bar as well as at a general shop.
- A trader only has so many cats; when they run out, sell elsewhere or buy something to give them cats back.
- Goods stolen from the shop's own people bring up a "Fencing Item" box with the chance of being caught: answer No and sell to another faction.
- Wiki trick for a big sale: first buy the trader's Skeleton Repair Kits to put cats into their purse, then resell the kits elsewhere.

## Trade window messages
Also: can't afford that, out of trading range, attempted smuggling, no room for that item, trade error
- "You can't afford that.": your cats are short for a purchase.
- "The shopkeeper can't afford that.": the trader's cats are short for your sale. Sell less, sell elsewhere, or buy something first.
- "Out of trading range.": the two inventories are too far apart; walk the character closer.
- "No room for that item.": the receiving grid has no free rectangle of that size.
- "Attempted smuggling!": the item is illegal in this town and this trader does not buy illegal goods.
- "That's not for sale.": the item is worn by the trader.
- An item sold and bought back before the trade window closes is reported to cost exactly what the sale paid, so mis-clicks can be undone at no loss.
- These strings come from notes on the game's code (meitouclient), not from the wiki.

## Shop money, opening hours and restock
Also: trader out of money, vendor cash, restock, refresh, shop inventory reset, shop closed at night
- Many shops (General Shop, Travel Gear) are closed to the public at night; bars and Waystation shops stay open.
- A trader's cats are generated from the value of the stock they hold. Game-code notes (meitouclient) give the rule: 25% of the stock's value, never below c.3,000 and never above c.25,000.
- So a small bar starts near c.3,000 and a shop full of expensive gear near c.25,000.
- Wandering traders not tied to a building are capped at c.25,000.
- Most shops restock goods and cats every 24 hours. Hive Traders restock every 6 hours, Thieves Guild traders every 12, the Scraphouse every 48.
- What you pay a trader goes into their purse, so do your buying first. The wiki suggests Skeleton Repair Kits (c.4,341) as a compact purchase that resells elsewhere with little loss.
- Travelling caravans that visit an outpost have no cats until you buy something from them.

## Mining copper: the safe first income
Also: copper mining, mine ore, ore node, outcrop, deposit, resource node
- Copper and iron outcrops stand in the open world; no tool, skill or research is needed to mine them.
- Select the character and right-click the outcrop to start mining (game-code notes: operating is the default right-click order on a mine or ore rock). Mined pieces pile up inside the node, not in the character's inventory.
- A node holds only 5 pieces; then work stops until someone empties it.
- To collect: hold right-click on the node and choose the loot option, or select the node and open the character's inventory, then move the ore across.
- A node takes 1 to 3 workers depending on its size.
- Mining trains Labouring; hauling the ore trains Strength.
- Mine copper, not iron: Raw Iron sells for half as much and weighs more than twice as much.
- Miners stand outside the walls, so watch for bandits and animals between advances.

## Copper (item)
Also: raw copper, copper ore, copper price, copper weight
- Name in game: Copper (formerly "Raw Copper").
- Weight 4 kg. Inventory size 3 wide by 2 tall.
- Average price c.180; normal range between towns c.126 to c.234.
- It is a trade good: you sell it at the full local price.
- Slaver-culture towns (Eyesocket, Port North, Port South, Slave Markets) pay 50% for Copper, Raw Iron, Iron Plates, Raw Stone and Building Materials.
- Used for Electrical Components, Copper Alloy Plates, Spring Steel, and building generators.
- Holy Nation shops never stock Copper.
- Only surface outcrops can be mined; underground copper shown by prospecting cannot be reached.
- kenshi.wiki's infobox shows "Base value c. 300": that is the raw data figure; in game it is multiplied by 0.6 for trade goods, giving c.180.

## Raw Iron (item)
Also: iron ore, raw iron price, iron weight
- Weight 9 kg. Inventory size 3 wide by 2 tall.
- Average price c.90.
- One Raw Iron makes one Iron Plates (2 kg, c.135) at an Iron Refinery.
- Four Raw Iron make one Armour Plating at a Plate Beating Station.
- Later it can be produced by an Ore Drill (25 Building Materials, needs power) instead of by hand.
- Per kilogram carried, Copper earns c.45 and Raw Iron c.10: sell iron only if you have nothing else to carry.

## Where to mine near The Hub
Also: Hub copper, Hub iron, Hub ore, ore near the Hub, copper near the Hub, starting mine
- The Border Zone is noted for many high-quality Iron and Copper veins close to towns with traders.
- Several copper and iron nodes are within walking distance of The Hub's gates; two more copper nodes are beside Rebel Base's bar, 0.4 km north.
- Player report: leave by the gate nearest the bar and go down the hill to find two copper nodes; another report places a copper node north-east of an iron node visible from the west gate (unverified)
- Use the Prospect button (lower right of the screen) to open a map showing iron and copper outcrops around the character.
- Prefer a node whose quality reads 100 or close to it.
- Sell at The Hub's bar (Black Market Bar, open all night).
- Squin, the other Border Zone town, also has Iron and Copper nearby, plus a General Shop.

## Mining yield and the Labouring skill
Also: ore per hour, mining speed, how fast mining, copper per day
- The wiki gives no rate. Game-data notes (meitouclient) do: a Copper node takes 2 workers and yields about 36 pieces per worker per in-game day at skill factor 1.0; an Iron node takes 3 workers at about 25.6 Raw Iron each.
- Skill factor = 0.5 + 0.9 x Labouring / 100: 0.5 at Labouring 0, 1.0 near 56, 1.4 at 100.
- Copper per worker per day: about 18 at Labouring 0, 36 at 56, 50 at 100. kenshi.zone's research notes give the same figures.
- At c.180 each that is roughly c.3,200 to c.9,000 per worker per day before travel and selling time; iron gives about c.1,150 to c.3,200.
- Output is multiplied by the node's quality percentage.
- A new character starts slow and speeds up as Labouring rises.
- Greenlanders have no Labouring penalty; Shek, Scorchlanders and Hive Princes learn it 20% slower; Hive Workers 20% faster.

## Carrying ore: weight and inventory limits
Also: encumbrance, carry weight, how much copper can I carry, overloaded
- Carry weight is Strength + 15 kg on the wiki (1.2 x Strength + 15 in meitouclient's in-game test); worn gear counts. A new character carries about 16 kg freely: roughly four Copper.
- Extra weight slows movement by 1% per 1% encumbrance and raises hunger rate, but trains Strength.
- The body inventory appears to be 8 by 10 squares (four 4x5 Beak Thing Eggs fill it), which fits ten 3x2 ore pieces unstacked (unverified)
- A worn Wooden Backpack (c.800, 8x8) halves the weight of its contents and lets goods that normally do not stack pile up to 9 per slot.
- A worn backpack lowers combat skills (Wooden: -5, 0.9x combat speed). If attacked, move it into the body inventory or drop it.
- A Pack Bull or Garru with an animal backpack (20x20, 90% weight reduction) removes the limit for trade runs.

## Automating mining with a storage box
Also: mining job, auto haul, ore storage, storage copper, shift click mine
- Buy a building near the ore. In The Hub a collapsed Small Shack costs c.1,500; it must be repaired with Building Materials before much fits inside.
- Build a Small Research Bench inside (3 Building Materials) and research "Storage Boxes: Ore" (no cost, under 1 hour).
- Build "Storage: Copper" or "Storage: Ore" (1 Iron Plates each; 100 Copper or 100 Raw Iron).
- Hold Shift and right-click the ore node to add mining to the character's job list (the wiki's Jobs page says right-click; its money guide says left-click). The miner then hauls ore to the matching storage unprompted.
- Jobs run only while the character's Jobs button is on, and are dropped if the character strays too far.
- Per game-code notes the node only works while its area is loaded: keep the camera or a character nearby.
- Empty the box into a backpack and sell it in one trip.

## Copper into Electrical Components
Also: electrical workbench, electrical crafting, value added copper
- An Electrical Workbench turns 1 Copper into 1 Electrical Components.
- Electrical Components: average price c.216, weight 1 kg (a quarter of Copper's weight), size 3x2.
- Gain: about c.36 per piece plus much lighter cargo, and it trains Science.
- Needs the Electrical Crafting research: Tech Level 3, 4 Books.
- The workbench costs 10 Iron Plates, is built indoors, needs 1 worker and draws 10 power.
- Put the workbench next to the copper storage so one hauler feeds it.

## Looting after other people's fights
Also: scavenging, loot bodies, loot bandits, loot unconscious, free gear
- New characters lose fights, so let others win them: town guards, bar guards and faction patrols.
- When hostile bandits or animals are down, right-click a body (or hold right-click and pick the loot action) to open its inventory and take what sells.
- Taking from members of a hostile bandit faction is not a crime, and every vendor accepts those items.
- Do not loot townspeople or guards: looting an unconscious person in town is a crime (c.500 bounty; c.2,500 for theft).
- One beaten bandit group is worth a few thousand cats.
- Wear the best armour you find, sell the rest.
- Bandage the downed afterwards: it trains Field Medic and slightly improves relations with their faction.
- Animals yield Raw Meat and Animal Skin.

## Luring bandits to town guards
Also: kiting, pull enemies to gate, guards kill bandits, bait
- Run past a hostile squad so it chases you, then run to a guarded town gate; the guards engage once you are close.
- Works with Dust Bandits, Starving Bandits and wildlife.
- The Hub has no major-faction gate guards: lure pursuers to the bar, whose Ninja Guards attack Dust Bandits, Starving Bandits and Bonedogs near it.
- If you have joined the Shinobi Thieves (c.10,000), bandits led to their tower in The Hub are killed quickly.
- Joining the fight beside guards is relatively safe attack training: step back when hit.
- Starving Bandits take food from downed characters and butcher downed animals.
- Looting of the downed: a forum report says Dust Bandits beat you down but do not loot, eat or enslave you (unverified). A code-based reference's list of squads that loot does not name them.

## What to take first when looting
Also: loot priority, most valuable loot, inventory space, carry more loot
- Compare Sell value, not Value: gear sells at 25% of Value.
- Rusted weapons are nearly worthless (an Unknown-quality Iron Club sells for about c.45). Catun-grade weapons sell for thousands (a Catun Katana c.2,361 to c.3,597).
- Dust Bandits wear Spiked Helmets, Heart Protectors, Armoured Rag Skirts and Samurai Boots; a Standard-grade Heart Protector sells for c.1,155, a Shoddy one for c.356.
- Take first aid kits and food for your own use.
- Long weapons (a Nodachi is 1x10) do not fit the body inventory; they go in a weapon slot or backpack.
- When full, pick up a knocked-out bandit, put loot in their inventory and carry them into the shop.
- A carried body adds 30 kg.

## Looting in Bast
Also: Bast battlefield, battlefield looting, war zone loot
- The Bast zone (ruined city of Bast, outpost Drin) has large United Cities and Holy Nation patrols that fight on sight.
- Wait until one whole side is down before touching anything; looting while they are up is a crime and they attack.
- Loot Holy Nation bodies: the nearby buyers (Drin, Stoat, Sho-Battai) are United Cities, so Holy Nation gear is not "stolen from them".
- Drin, in north-east Bast, has a bar to sell at and free beds.
- Holy Nation soldiers who attack Drin gain a c.500 Assault bounty, rising by c.500 each time they get back up (to about c.1,500). Two prisoner cages on a Drin rooftop pay the bounty when you put them in.
- When Drin's barman is out of cats, carry the rest to Stoat or Sho-Battai, avoiding Skimmers.

## Loot with a uniform or stolen tag: what can be sold
Also: looted gear tags, stolen tag on loot, can't sell stolen, laundering loot
- Looted gear with the faction uniform tag: do not wear it ("Wearing faction uniforms (Uniform Theft)" in Law and crime). Faction uniform pieces sell at 30% of the plain item's price, and a trader can refuse one ("What is that a uniform? I don't want that!").
- Gear taken from a neutral or allied faction is flagged stolen and names the owner for a while.
- Selling stolen goods to any other faction, to hivers, or to the Shinobi Thieves always succeeds.
- Selling them back to the faction they came from succeeds with chance 20% + 0.42% per Thievery level.
- The stolen flag is removed by putting the item into a specialised storage container you own (Armour Storage for armour, a Crossbow Locker for anything) or an Item Furnace and taking it out.
- The Hub's bar buys stolen goods unless they were stolen from the bar itself.

## Small bounties on common bandits
Also: random bounty, dust boss bounty, bounty hunting early, wanted bandit
- Select any character and look at the top of the bottom-left panel: a bounty is shown there; hover over it to see which faction pays.
- Some ordinary enemies spawn with a bounty. Dust Boss: c.2,000 to c.3,000, 25% chance, paid by United Cities. Sand Ninja Jounin: c.6,000 to c.10,000, always. Outlaw Swordsman Captain, Rebel Captain: c.3,000 to c.3,500, always. Escaped Slave: c.2,000, always.
- Bar thugs in The Hub, Shark and Waystations occasionally carry c.500 to c.2,500.
- Let someone else knock them out, bandage them (a corpse pays half), pick them up and carry them to the paying faction's police.
- Attacking a character who has a bounty brings no trouble with police.
- Strip their gear and sell it separately before handing them in.

## Turning in a bounty
Also: turning in bounties, hand in bounty, claim bounty, bounty hunting, deliver to police, collect reward
- Hold right-click on the body and choose "pick up", then carry them to a Police Station of a faction that wants them.
- Either talk to the Police Chief (United Cities), Hundred Guardian (Shek Kingdom) or Inquisitor (Holy Nation) while carrying the body, or right-click a cage in the station ("put in"). On payment the game shows "Received c.(amount) reward."
- Alive pays the full reward; dead pays half.
- Handing over through dialogue also gives about +2 relations with that faction (Holy Nation +5 if you decline payment); caging them yourself gives only the cats.
- A bounty also pays out if you get the prisoner into a cage belonging to the faction, even without staff present.
- In the Holy Nation only a human male can hand in through dialogue; anyone can use the cage.
- Strip the prisoner's gear and sell it first; the chief otherwise resells it.
- Wanted Posters (sold in bars and police stations) are not required to collect.

## Which faction pays which bounty
Also: who pays a bounty, bounty faction, where to turn in, paying faction
- Most bandit bounties (Dust Boss, Sand Ninja, outlaws, bar thugs) are United Cities bounties, so from the Border Zone they must be carried a long way; check the paying faction before picking a body up.
- A few named targets are paid by the Shek Kingdom (for example Ghost, Flying Bull, Shade, Tora) or the Holy Nation (Buzan, Dimak, Seto).
- The Hub has no police station; Squin (Shek Kingdom) and Stack (Holy Nation) are the nearest. The full list of towns with one: "Police Stations: which towns have one" in Towns.
- Police stations have training dummies; using them without being allied annoys the residents.

## Selling captives to slavers
Also: selling people, sell prisoners, sell bandits, Slaver Boss, sell to Rebirth
- Slave Shops (Slave Traders faction) buy unconscious bandit-type characters such as Starving Vagrants, Rebel Farmers, Sand Ninjas and Bloodraiders for about c.400 each. Game-code notes put the payout at c.200 to c.2,500 by the captive's stats.
- Bandage the captive, carry them in and talk to the Slaver Boss. Caging them yourself there enslaves them but pays only their bounty, if any (wiki).
- Only bandits, slaves, adventurers and civilians can be sold; soldiers (Paladins, Samurai, Shek Warriors), Black Dragon Ninjas and Pacifiers cannot.
- Slave Shops: Bark, Eyesocket, Heft, Port North, Port South, Sho-Battai, Slave Farm, Slave Markets, South Stone Camp, Stone Camp, Stoat, Trader's Edge. They stay open all night.
- Rebirth's shopkeeper pays c.500 for Starving Bandits, Holy Nation Outlaws, cannibals or fishmen if you ask for payment, otherwise +1 Holy Nation relations; a human male must do the talking.
- Risk: in slaver country your own characters, if knocked out, may be enslaved by Manhunters, Slave Hunters or Slave Traders.

## Trading between towns: how prices work
Also: trade route, markup, buy low sell high, wandering trader, price markup
- Each trade good has a base value. Every town rolls its own price within plus or minus 30% of base, fixed for that save.
- The item tooltip lists a price mark-up percentage for the current town: below 100% is cheap, above is dear.
- Trade goods sell for exactly what they cost locally, so profit is the difference between two towns' markups.
- Neighbouring towns have similar markups (Stack to Bad Teeth changes little); far-apart towns differ much more (Stack to Heng).
- Method: buy whatever shows the lowest markup, sell wherever it is higher, rebuy the cheapest goods there.
- Trade-good shops: General Shop, Empire General Shop, Hive Trader, Adventurer Mechanical Shop.
- A stacking backpack (Wooden or Trader's Wooden) multiplies how much fits.
- The area around The Hub is close to many towns, which suits early trading.

## Regional price modifiers
Also: trade culture, where prices are higher, slaver discount, swamp prices
- On top of the random 30%, some factions apply fixed multipliers.
- Slave Trader towns (Eyesocket, Port North, Port South, Slave Markets): Copper, Raw Iron, Iron Plates, Raw Stone, Building Materials at 50%; Wheatstraw, Cactus, Cotton at 40%; Sake 150%; Bloodrum and Hemp 400%; Hashish 600%.
- Swamp towns (Mud Town, Shark, Swamp Village) and the Smugglers Bar: Sake, Gohan, Hashish, Hemp, Riceweed at 75%; Cactus Rum, Bloodrum, Bread, Foodcube, Meatwrap at 125%; Wheatstraw 20%.
- United Cities towns: Sake 150%, Bloodrum 400% (illegal), Hashish 1200% (illegal), Hemp 400% (illegal).
- Shek Kingdom towns: Hashish 350% (illegal).
- Holy Nation towns: Hashish 400% (illegal).
- Grog has no regional modifier anywhere.
- So: never sell ore in slaver towns, and buy building supplies there.

## Alcohol trade loop
Also: rum route, sake route, grog, booze trading
- Cactus Rum (base c.520) sells at 125% in the Swamp. The wiki's routes buy it at The Hub, Squin and the Stenn Desert Waystation, although the Hub bar's listed stock does not include it.
- Sake (base c.428): 75% in the Swamp, 150% in United Cities and slaver towns. Buy in the Swamp, sell in United Cities bars.
- Grog (base c.1,155): no fixed modifier; buy wherever the markup is low and sell where it is high.
- A wiki contributor's three-stop loop (Hub: buy Cactus Rum; Swamp: sell it, buy Sake; United Cities: sell Sake, buy Grog; Hub: sell Grog) made about c.7,000 per circuit.
- Bloodrum (base c.1,328) sells at 400% in United Cities but is contraband there; it is legal in Holy Nation and Shek towns.
- The Swamp has Blood Spiders and Swamp Ninjas, and gate guards may be corrupt: this is not a solo day-one route.

## Hashish: where to buy and sell
Also: hash, drugs, drug running, smuggling route
- Base value c.144; weight 1 kg; size 3x2.
- Buy: Fish and Drugs Sellers in every Swamp Village, Mud Town and Shark, and Hive Traders in Hive Villages. The wiki lists the Swamp price as 75% of base; meitouclient's data read says 100%.
- Legal, any-trader sale: Flats Lagoon, Mourn and the Manhunter Base. The Hashish page says 400% there; the trade-culture page says 500%.
- Illegal markets: United Cities northern towns (Heft, Bark, Heng, Sho-Battai, Stoat) 1200%; Clownsteady, Drifter's Last, Catun 500%; slaver towns 600%; Holy Nation 400%; Shek 350%.
- In illegal towns only the Shinobi Trader in the Thieves Guild tower buys it (membership c.10,000), at half the local price: effectively 600% in Heft, Heng and Sho-Battai.
- The Swamp to Flats Lagoon run passes Swamp Ninjas, Blood Spiders and Beak Things.
- Hashish can also be made: 5 Hemp + 1 Water at a Hemp Processor.

## Smuggling past gate guards
Also: contraband, bag check, illegal items, gate search
- Town gate guards may stop a character and ask to search for contraband. Few items are contraband, so early on simply agree.
- Refusing a bag check or attacking the guard is a crime (c.4,000 bounty in United Cities).
- Illegal in United Cities: Hashish, Bloodrum, Hemp. In Shek towns: Hashish. In Holy Nation towns: Hashish, Ancient Science Books, AI Cores and certain books.
- Wiki trick: put the illegal goods inside a backpack, then put that backpack into the body inventory instead of wearing it; the search does not find them.
- Hemp Farms and Hemp Processors are illegal buildings in United Cities land; a Samurai who sees one starts a Drug Bust (c.2,000 bounty or a bribe).
- Non-human characters are attacked in Holy Nation territory regardless of cargo.

## Hunting animals for meat and skins
Also: hunting, raw meat, animal skin, leather, feed the squad
- Raw Meat (c.60, 15 nutrition): Garru 10, Wild Bull 10, Goat 7, Crab 6, Beak Thing 5, Bonedog 3, Skimmer 2, Swamp Turtle 14 per corpse.
- Cook Raw Meat into Dried Meat at a Campfire or Cooking Stove, one for one. Dried Meat sells for c.39, so meat is food, not income.
- Animal Skin (average price c.240): Beak Thing 6, Gorillo 6, Wild Bull 4, Garru 3, Goat 2, Bonedog 1, River Raptor 1.
- One Leather needs 0.5 Animal Skin at a Leather Tanning Bench; Leather averages c.156.
- Animal Claw c.90, Animal Horn c.150, Animal Teeth c.30.
- Cheapest way: let gate guards kill animals (Bad Teeth's guards often kill River Raptors) and loot the corpses.
- A character can be given the job of looting dead animals.

## Beak Thing Eggs
Also: egg stealing, nest raid, Beak Thing nest
- Beak Thing Egg: c.4,200 at every trader, 6 kg, size 4x5. Four fill a body inventory (24 kg). Ten eggs are c.42,000.
- Eggs lie on the ground in a Nest of Beak Things; nests spawn randomly in many zones, notably Vain and Gut.
- Send one fast or stealthy character at night, sneak on, grab eggs, run. Do not fight the Beak Things.
- Beak Things outrun most characters, but their attack animation is slow.
- Beak Things eat knocked-out characters alive: failure usually means death.
- Useful kit: a backpack (Thieves Backpacks carry no stealth penalty), Wooden Sandals for speed.
- Gut's nests are near Brink and Heng, which buy the eggs.
- Crab Eggs (c.240) are gathered the same way for far less.

## Looting ruins and ancient locations
Also: ruins, ancient lab, scavenge ruins, artifacts, tech loot
- Unguarded ruins near starts hold sellable junk: Waystation Ruins (Venge, Stobe's Garden) have Skeleton Repair Kits (c.4,341), Steel Bars, Building Materials, sometimes Skeleton Eyes (c.3,600) and a Power Core (c.3,000).
- The Old Village in The Great Desert and the intact houses in Bast have low-level loot and sometimes backpacks.
- Near The Hub: Tiny Settlement and Ruined Holy Outpost; sell at the Border Zone Waystation.
- Ancient Labs and Lost Armouries hold Research Artifacts and high-value parts (CPU Unit c.6,000, Robotics Components c.2,838) but are guarded, often by Security Spiders; each spider drops Skeleton Muscles worth about c.1,944.
- Bring a stacking backpack or pack animal.
- Many containers are locked: bring a lockpicker.
- Keep Books, Ancient Science Books and Engineering Research for your own research.

## Crafting for sale: what pays
Also: crafting income, armour smith money, weapon smith money, best item to craft
- Crafted gear sells at 25% of its Value, and Value rises steeply with grade, so profit depends on the crafter's skill.
- Bandana (Clothing Bench, 0.4 Fabrics, about 0.6 hours): sells c.6 Prototype, c.46 Shoddy, c.175 Standard, c.391 High, c.692 Specialist, c.975 Masterwork. Fabrics cost about c.63.
- Bandanas need the Clothing Manufacture and Hats and Headgear research (2 Books for the latter).
- From roughly Armour Smith 40, switch to Heart Protectors (sell c.356 Shoddy, c.1,155 Standard, c.2,487 High); the wiki calls them the best crafting earner. They need an Engineering Research (c.8,000 at the Scraphouse).
- Desert Sabres: cost about c.675 in materials on a basic Weapon Smithing Bench and sell from c.942 at the lowest quality; the blueprint costs about c.12,000.
- Crossbow making is the least profitable branch.

## Brewing and refining for sale
Also: grog machine, sake still, rum still, steel bars, chainmail sheets, repair kits
- Grog: 20 Wheatstraw + 1 Water at a Grog Machine; sells c.1,155. Buying the inputs costs about c.825, so home-grown Wheatstraw is the point.
- Sake: 20 Riceweed + 1 Water at a Sake Still; base c.428, 150% in United Cities.
- Cactus Rum: 20 Cactus + 1 Water at a Rum Still; base c.520.
- Steel Bars (c.648): 4 Iron Plates at a Steel Refinery (3 at higher levels). A fully automated iron chain is quoted at about 51 bars a day per refinery.
- Skeleton Repair Kit (c.4,341): 0.5 Robotics Components (c.2,838 each) + 1 Steel Bars; profit about c.2,274 each; needs the Robotics research (1 Ancient Science Book).
- Chainmail Sheets (c.2,527): 3 Steel Bars; profit about c.584; needs Tech Level 4.
- Most industry buildings are exterior-only, so they need an outpost, not a town house.

## Theft: how stealing works
Also: stealing, thievery, steal from shop, shoplifting, pick up red items
- Items with red names are owned. Picking one up needs Alt held and counts as theft.
- Loose items on floors and shelves: 100% success if nobody sees you. Containers: chance depends on Thievery and how close the nearest conscious owner is (game-code notes: at least 30%, rising with skill and distance). Failure shows "Got caught stealing."
- Stealing from a character's own inventory: about 1.1% per Thievery level.
- Sneak first (Sneak button). After a successful steal, stay unseen for a few seconds until the timer above the character's name (bottom left) runs out.
- Shop method: enter in sneak, hide behind furniture, wait for night until the shopkeeper locks up and sleeps; take shelf items first, containers later.
- Locked containers always hold something; unlocked ones say [EMPTY] if empty.
- Practise on junk in houses: being caught there does not cost you a shop.

## Theft: risks and penalties
Also: caught stealing, theft bounty, jail, banned from shop, crime
- Caught stealing from a shop: that shop will never trade with your faction again, whichever character you send.
- Bounties when seen: Trespassing c.100, Looting c.500, Assault c.500, Burglary (lockpicking) c.1,000, Theft c.2,500, Escaping Prison c.2,500, Kidnapping or freeing prisoners c.5,000.
- Bounties expire: 4 hours per c.100. Jail time, if captured or surrendering, is half that.
- A total above c.10,000 makes the character "notorious": the bounty no longer expires.
- Police and bounty hunters recognise wanted characters fastest; a bigger bounty is recognised by more people.
- In slaver territory, a jailed character can be sold into slavery.
- Entering a private building or staying after closing is trespassing: owners warn you several times in daytime first.
- Crimes in the wilderness, with no major faction watching, are not reported.

## Theft: what to steal and where to sell it
Also: fence, stolen goods buyer, launder stolen items
- Best value for the risk: blueprints and alcohol from shops.
- Stolen goods sell for half value, and the faction they came from may catch you.
- Sell to a different faction in the same town (many towns host several), to Tech Hunter Waystations, to hivers, or to The Hub's bar.
- Thief Fences sit in random bars and trade with anyone at 50% prices, no questions.
- Launder: place the goods in your own specialised storage (or an Item Furnace, or a Crossbow Locker) and take them out; they then sell at full price.
- Shinobi Trader trick: sell stolen goods to them, buy them back, and they are no longer stolen.
- Luxury Goods (c.240) sit in Noble Houses.

## Fog Prince heads and Leviathan Pearls
Also: late game money, fog prince, leviathan
- Fog Prince Head: c.6,000 at any vendor, no police needed. Fog Princes come with Fogmen attacking Mongrel; its guards kill them, and Princes bleed yellow.
- Being downed in the Fog Islands is extremely dangerous; the wiki advises early squads not to hunt there.
- Leviathan Pearl: c.12,000, one per Leviathan (Leviathan Coast). Leviathans are the toughest ordinary creatures; young ones drop the same pearl.
- The wiki's method is a walled base with many turrets facing the gate.
- Megacrab Ganglion sells for c.3,000.
- None of this is for a squad that still needs mining income.

## Selling from your own shop counter
Also: player shop, shop counter, public sales, customers
- Research Public Sales (Tech Level 1 Core tech), then build a Shop Counter (4 Building Materials, indoors).
- Put goods in the counter. If the building and the town are open to the public, passing NPCs buy from it.
- Customers have little money: residents carry c.0 to c.500, drifters c.200 to c.2,000, caravan bosses c.2,000 to c.8,000, and residents' cats are not replenished.
- They buy only certain types: mostly food and narcotics, some first aid, splints, tools and misc.
- Treat it as a trickle, not a main income.
