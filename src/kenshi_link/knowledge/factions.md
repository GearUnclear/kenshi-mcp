# Factions

Who the factions of Kenshi are, where they live, how they treat a weak new squad, what changes their opinion of you, and what happens when their leaders fall.

## Faction relations: scale and thresholds
Also: reputation, relationship number, hostile, allied, enemy, neutral, why are they attacking me
- Every faction holds one relation number toward your whole player faction, from -100 to +100.
- At -30 or lower the faction is an enemy: its members attack on sight and gate guards attack instead of letting you in.
- At +50 or higher the faction is allied.
- Between those values the faction is neutral: you can enter its towns and trade.
- Most factions start at 0. Some game starts change this (see "Starting relations by game start").
- Bandit factions and Manhunters may attack even while the number says neutral.
- Anyone who sees you commit a crime against their faction or its allies may attack, even if the faction is allied to you.
- Allying with one faction does not by itself make its rivals hostile; only specific pledges or handing in rival leaders do.

## Checking your relations in game
Also: faction window, faction overview, where to see reputation
- The Factions overview lists each known faction with a meter and a whole number.
- Hovering over a visited settlement on the map shows your relation with its owner to several decimal places, useful for seeing small changes.
- Allied factions' locations are drawn green on the map.
- Factions such as Drifters, Slaves, Fogmen and wildlife never appear in the list and their relation cannot be changed.
- Which button opens the Factions overview on your screen: find it in the window buttons and note it (unverified)

## What lowers relations
Also: losing reputation, why a faction turned hostile
- Attacking a faction's members lowers relations; the drop is faster for higher-ranking members.
- Long fights against many members can turn even a neutral faction hostile.
- Putting a character in a Prisoner Cage and then talking to them: -1 with their faction.
- Attacking, imprisoning or killing a character with Diplomatic Status (leaders, nobles, Pacifiers) usually damages relations severely.
- Being caught stealing costs a little standing with the owner's faction.
- Handing a faction leader to that faction's enemy as a bounty: typically -75 with the leader's faction.
- Dialogue pledges: joining the Anti-Slavers through Grey gives -30 with United Cities, Traders Guild and Slave Traders; joining Rebel Farmers through Boss Simion gives -25 with United Cities and Slave Traders.

## What raises relations
Also: gaining reputation, improving standing, making friends
- Bandaging or repairing a hurt member raises relations a little; about 0.01 per 100 points healed on an ordinary member, far more on a leader.
- Healing can only lift relations up to +5, enough to stop attacks on sight but not to ally.
- You do not need to be seen healing for it to count.
- Handing a bounty to the police chief through dialogue: +2 with that faction per bounty, whatever its size, alive or dead.
- The Holy Nation gives +5 instead of +2 if you decline payment; only a human male can hand in there.
- Buying a slave: +4 with the slave's own faction and +1 with Slave Traders.
- Paying a Pacifier repairs negative relations (see "Pacifiers").
- Some factions sell alliances outright: United Cities via Koin, Traders Guild via Yamdu, Shinobi Thieves via a Thief Boss.

## Pacifiers: what they do and what they cost
Also: fix bad relations, pay off a faction, peace, reset hostility
- Pacifiers are characters in bars who repair negative relations with their faction for cats.
- Shek Kingdom, Slave Traders, The Holy Nation, Traders Guild, United Cities: c.15,000 if below -10, c.35,000 below -20, c.55,000 below -40, c.75,000 below -60, c.100,000 below -80.
- Anti-Slavers, Flotsam Ninjas, Hounds: c.10,000 / 20,000 / 30,000 / 40,000 / 50,000 for the same bands.
- Each payment lifts you only to the top of the next band, so very bad relations need several payments.
- After one payment the Pacifier is "done for the day" for 2 hours.
- A Pacifier refuses if you killed or imprisoned that faction's leaders or are allied with its enemies; he says why.
- Pacifiers have Diplomatic Status, so they still talk to you when their faction is hostile.

## Pacifier locations
Also: where is the pacifier, Holy Pacifier, Shek Pacifier, UC Pacifier
- The Holy Nation: Shark.
- Shek Kingdom: World's End.
- United Cities: Flats Lagoon.
- Traders Guild: Blister Hill.
- Slave Traders: Stoat.
- Hounds: The Hub.
- Flotsam Ninjas: Squin.
- Anti-Slavers: Mud Town.
- The Holy Nation, Shek Kingdom, Traders Guild and United Cities Pacifiers each also have a 25% chance to be at a Waystation.

## Becoming allies: what an alliance gives
Also: ally benefits, +50 relations, alliance perks
- Reaching +50 makes you allied automatically.
- Allied gate guards stop searching your bags.
- Allied members heal your downed characters, sometimes hand over food or medicine, and fight on your side against factions they are not allied to.
- Allied members ignore bounties on your characters, but still react to crimes they witness.
- An allied faction may send help when your outpost is attacked.
- You may wear an allied faction's uniform, and use its beds and training dummies, without trouble.
- Factions that can be allied through dialogue: Anti-Slavers, Crab Raiders, Flotsam Ninjas, Hounds, Rebel Farmers, Shek Kingdom, Shinobi Thieves, Skeleton Bandits, Skin Bandits, The Holy Nation, Traders Guild, United Cities.

## Diplomatic Status characters
Also: Diplomat status, diplomats, important people, why crossbows will not target
- Leaders, nobles and Pacifiers carry Diplomatic Status; harming or imprisoning them usually brings their whole faction down on you.
- Your ranged characters refuse to shoot them unless they attacked first.
- They can still be spoken to when their faction is hostile (this is how Pacifiers work).
- Healing a wounded leader with high faction importance can pull relations from -100 back to +5 within seconds.
- Enemy police chiefs accept characters with Diplomatic Status as bounties with no cash reward.

## Killing or capturing faction leaders
Also: world states, town overrides, destroying a faction
- Killing or imprisoning a leader sets a World State; towns tied to that leader can be replaced by a ruined version or handed to another faction.
- A replaced town is a new location: buildings you bought there are lost.
- That faction's roaming squads become rarer and other factions start to spawn in its lands.
- Not something a weak squad can do; leaders sit among dozens of elite guards.
- See the sections on each major faction for the specific outcomes.

## Who is dangerous around The Hub
Also: Hub threats, starting area enemies, early game dangers
- The Border Zone has no regular army patrols, so bandits roam freely.
- Most common roaming squads: Starving Bandits (5 to 16 Hungry Bandits), Dust Bandits (1 Dust Boss, 5 to 10 Dust Bandits, 0 to 4 Dust Bandit Bowmen), Slave Traders squads of 1 to 8 Slavemongers, Escaped Servants (1 to 8).
- Animals: Bonedogs (lone, or packs from a Wolf Den), wild Goats, Garrus.
- Neutral travellers: a lone Drifter, Nomads with pack animals, a Wandering Assassin.
- Strongholds to stay away from: Dust King Tower, Tower Of Ninjas and any Dust Bandit Camp.
- The wiki calls this the mildest zone in the game: bandits knock you out and take food rather than kill or eat you; passing Slavemongers may still heal and enslave a downed character.
- Vain to the west (Beak Things) and The Swamp to the south (Blood Spiders) are far deadlier.

## The Hub: owners and protection
Also: who owns the Hub, who runs the Hub, Hub faction, Hub owners
- The Hub is a mostly ruined town in the middle of the Border Zone, nominally owned by Holy Nation Outlaws.
- Its buildings are a Bar, a Thieves Guild tower and one Small Shack; there is no Police Station.
- It has no town guards: the wiki says every major town except The Hub has them.
- The bar is owned by the Trade Ninjas; their Ninja Guards (2 to 4) attack Dust Bandits, Starving Bandits and Bonedogs that come near, and anyone who causes trouble in the bar.
- The Shinobi Thieves hold a tower (Thieves Guild) at the far end of town; if you are allied with them they run out and defend you.
- The Hounds Pacifier stands in The Hub.
- One intact Small Shack has two beds, but residents may attack if they see you inside.
- Nearby Rebel Base is a one-house Black Market Bar, also guarded by Trade Ninjas, with two copper nodes beside it.

## Starving Bandits (Hungry Bandits)
Also: starvers, Hungry Bandit Leader, food thieves, beggars, steal my food
- Hostile to everyone; found all around Holy Nation lands, very common in the Border Zone and Okran's Gulf.
- They attack unprovoked, or the leader walks up and begs or demands food: giving c.50 changes nothing, c.500 gives +2 relations, c.1,500 gives +5; a refusal makes the whole squad attack.
- A group you paid enough becomes friendly and defends you nearby; if allied, they bodyguard for 24 hours when given food.
- They loot unconscious characters for food only, leave weapons and armour, then move on.
- They butcher unconscious animals for meat, so a downed pet dies.
- They cannot take food lying on the ground, in a container, or in an unworn backpack kept in the inventory: drop your food before going down and pick it up afterwards.
- Can be sold to Rebirth for c.500 each or handed over for +1 Holy Nation relations.
- Squad sizes and combat stats: "Starving Bandit squads: size, stats and gear" in Wildlife and hazards.

## Dust Bandits
Also: Dust Boss, bandit toll, demand money, pay for safe passage, mugging
- Hostile bandits of the Border Zone, Shem and Skinner's Roam, led by the Dust King.
- The Dust Boss of a squad may talk first and demand cats for safe passage.
- Paying c.500 ends it peacefully if the speaker wears no backpack; offering c.300 makes him ask for more.
- If the speaker has a backpack equipped he assumes more money is hidden and keeps demanding even after c.500.
- Refusing starts a fight. Speaking to them first and answering "No" to a fight can avoid hostilities.
- A Dust Boss has a 25% chance of carrying a c.2,000 to c.3,000 bounty.
- Looting of the downed: a forum report says Dust Bandits beat you down but do not loot, eat or enslave you (unverified). A code-based reference's list of squads that loot does not name them.
- Squad sizes, combat stats and gear: "Dust Bandit squads: size, stats and gear" in Wildlife and hazards.

## Dust King (Dust Bandits leader)
Also: Dust Bandits boss, Dust King bounty, bounty 35,000
- The Dust King leads the Dust Bandits from Dust King Tower in the Border Zone; the tower, its garrison and how to get in are under "Dust King Tower (hostile outpost)" in Towns.
- Bounty c.35,000 (his Wanted Poster says c.30,000), payable by United Cities, The Holy Nation or Shek Kingdom.
- Killing or imprisoning him severely damages Dust Bandit relations and stops their "Bandit Demands" outpost event.
- The unique recruit Cat is held prisoner in his tower.
- Wiki tip for later: hire Mercenaries at the nearby Waystation for a one-day contract and let them fight the tower.

## Holy Nation Outlaws and Escaped Servants
Also: escaped holy servants, outlaws, Escaped Servant
- A small faction of people on the run from The Holy Nation; they nominally own The Hub and Rebel Base.
- Where Escaped Servants roam: Border Zone, Okran's Gulf, Okran's Pride, Okran's Valley, Skinner's Roam, Vain, Dreg, Fog Islands, Cannibal Plains; they also patrol out of The Hub.
- Groups of 1 to 8, starving, combat stats 15.
- Not hostile by default, but they may attack a nearby squad out of hunger, less readily than Hungry Bandits.
- They loot food from unconscious characters and butcher animals.
- They react verbally to Skeletons the way the Holy Nation does but do not attack on sight unless already your enemy.
- A lone, badly injured wandering Escaped Servant who speaks to you first can be recruited for free.
- Luring them into a Holy Nation town gets them a bounty you can then hand in.

## Trade Ninjas
Also: Trade Ninja guards, Ninja Guards, trade ninja faction
- Small faction that owns the bars in The Hub, Rebel Base and Mourn.
- Their Ninja Guards attack bandits and Bonedogs near the bar and anyone starting trouble inside.
- Their Black Market Bar buys stolen goods from any faction except goods stolen from the Trade Ninjas themselves.
- At Rebel Base the guards side with law enforcers such as Holy Nation paladins against you.
- Leading too many bandits to them can get the guards and the barman killed.

## Black Dragon Ninjas
Also: Black Dragons, protection racket, ninja extortion
- Hostile ninjas based in the Tower Of Ninjas in the Border Zone; they attack anyone who approaches the tower.
- Led by Dimak and Buzan (c.20,000 bounty each from The Holy Nation); the tower also holds at least 10 Black Dragon Genin.
- They raid player outposts built within 4,500 metres of the tower while Dimak is alive.
- They count as military, not bandits, so they cannot be sold to slavers.

## The Holy Nation: overview and territory
Also: HN, Okranites, Holy Nation lands, Holy Nation towns
- Theocratic, all-Greenlander state north of the Border Zone; the most populous faction.
- Capital: Blister Hill (Holy Lord Phoenix). Other towns: Stack (on the edge of their land, close to The Hub; High Inquisitor Seta) and Bad Teeth.
- Other sites: Holy Farm villages, Holy Mines, Holy Military Base, Okran's Fist, Okran's Shield, Narko's Trap, Rebirth (slave camp).
- Patrols roam Okran's Pride, Okran's Gulf, Okran's Valley, Bast and Skimsands; the wiki lists them in the Border Zone only after certain leaders die.
- Enemies (-100): Shek Kingdom, United Cities, Flotsam Ninjas, Holy Nation Outlaws, Mongrel. Dislikes Anti-Slavers (-50). Neutral with the Western Hive.
- Starts neutral (0) to a default new game.
- Patrol sizes: Holy Army Patrol is 1 High Paladin, 4 to 6 Paladins, 6 to 10 Holy Servants; Inquisitor Roam is 1 Inquisitor, 4 High Paladins, 4 Paladins.

## The Holy Nation: races, women and Skeletons
Also: non-humans in the Holy Nation, xenophobia, Holy Nation and female characters, who attacks Skeletons
- Human males are welcomed; patrols may heal them or hand them food.
- Skeletons are attacked on sight unless you are allied (+50).
- Any character wearing a robotic replacement limb is treated like a Skeleton and attacked.
- Shek and Hive characters without a human in the same squad close by are arrested as trespassers and sent to Rebirth.
- Women are tolerated and may trade, but officials will not deal with them: no handing in bounties or prisoners, and a Holy Priest may question them. Insulting the priest leads to arrest and Rebirth.
- Scorchlanders are human, so they pass.
- Characters being carried are ignored: a human can carry a Skeleton or robot-limbed character through Holy Nation land.
- Do not mention Narko to guards and do not try to bribe them.

## Holy Nation interrogations: what to answer
Also: stopped by paladins, High Paladin questions, interrogation, questioned by a patrol
- Unallied squads can be pulled aside by a patrol for questioning as suspected foreigners.
- With The Holy Flame in the speaker's inventory, a human pacifies the interrogation every time.
- Without the book, pick the most loyal answers (swear loyalty to Okran or the Phoenix, bow, chant); a small chance of attack remains.
- Praising Narko makes Paladins attack.
- A High Paladin sometimes walks up to a human male and gives him an item such as a Ration Pack.
- Passing patrols may heal injured human characters or feed starving ones.
- Being knocked out near a slave camp such as Holy Mines or Rebirth can still end in enslavement.
- Once allied (+50) there are no interrogations.
- Patrol sizes and where they march: "Holy Nation patrols" in Wildlife and hazards.

## Bringing Shek or Hive characters into Holy Nation land
Also: non-human escort, getting non-humans through Holy Nation territory, human escort, "beasts", servants
- Patrols do not attack Shek or Hive characters who stay close to a human of their own squad; guards take them for servants.
- With them along, roughly 2 in 5 patrols stop you for interrogation. Let a human male carrying The Holy Flame do the talking.
- If the High Paladin speaks to a human first, claim they are already your slaves or servants and he leaves; if he speaks to the Shek or Hive character first, that character must surrender as a slave or the patrol turns hostile.
- When guards demand your non-humans, the right answers plus The Holy Flame keep them 91% of the time for a male speaker and 50% for a female.
- At town gates, walk the Shek or Hive up to the Holy Sentinel first with the humans right behind; presented this way they are never arrested.
- Skeletons and robot-limbed characters cannot be escorted, only carried by an organic character.
- Once allied, non-humans need no escort. Otherwise the wiki calls bringing them in a very bad idea.

## The Holy Flame (book)
Also: Holy Flame, holy book, bible, prayer book
- The Holy Flame is the Holy Nation's religious book; by their law every citizen carries one.
- It is found everywhere in Holy Nation towns.
- Carried by the character who does the talking, it gets you out of most encounters with Paladins and Inquisitors.
- It is required to complete Prayer Day at an outpost; the Holy Priest gives one copy free the first time if you have none.
- Human males carrying it are searched least often at Holy Nation gates.
- It is not sold in Shek Kingdom, United Cities or Tech Hunter shops.

## The Holy Nation: raising relations and allying
Also: ally Holy Nation, Holy Nation reputation, befriend the Holy Nation
- A human male handing bounties to an Inquisitor gets +5 each if he asks for no payment, +2 if he takes the money.
- About 10 unpaid hand-ins reach the +50 alliance.
- Handing bandits to Rebirth without payment gives +1 each (about 50 needed).
- Handing in Moll gives +80; Esata the Stone Golem +80; Seto +50; Tinfist +50.
- Telling Moll at Flotsam Village that you are loyal to the Phoenix: +50 Holy Nation, -75 Flotsam Ninjas.
- Once allied: Skeletons and robot-limbed characters are no longer attacked, non-humans need no escort, you may wear their uniform and enter Narko's Trap.
- Once allied, Shek Kingdom squads attack you unprovoked.
- If you are allied with Anti-Slavers or Flotsam Ninjas, Inquisitors attack when spoken to.

## Holy Nation leaders and what happens if they fall
Also: Holy Lord Phoenix, High Inquisitor Seta, High Inquisitor Valtena, Holy Nation collapse
- Holy Lord Phoenix: throne room in Blister Hill with 14 High Paladins and the Protector of the Flame. Bounty c.100,000 from the Shek Kingdom or United Cities.
- High Inquisitor Seta: Stack. High Inquisitor Valtena: Okran's Shield. Bounty c.40,000 each.
- Seta killed or imprisoned: Stack and Bad Teeth go to the Shek Kingdom if Esata lives, else to Kral's Chosen if Flying Bull lives.
- Phoenix killed or imprisoned: Blister Hill is destroyed, Holy Military Bases fall to Fogmen, Prayer Day stops, Inquisitor dialogue locks.
- Valtena killed or imprisoned: Okran's Shield falls; patrols thin out in Bast and Okran's Valley and stop in Skimsands.
- All three gone: Strayed Paladins replace normal patrols; Blister Hill and Rebirth go to the Flotsam Ninjas if Moll lives, otherwise to Holy Nation Outlaws.
- Handing the Phoenix to an enemy faction: -75 Holy Nation.

## Shek Kingdom: overview and territory
Also: Shek faction, Shek lands, Shek towns, Hundred Guardian
- Warrior kingdom of the horned Shek, centred on the Stenn Desert, which borders the Border Zone.
- Capital: Admag (hilltop, one entrance). Other holdings: Squin (in a canyon in the Border Zone, directly east of Admag), Last Stand, The Great Fortress.
- Patrols (1 to 2 Hundred Guardians with 4 to 8 Shek Warriors) roam Stenn Desert and Spider Plains.
- Enemy of The Holy Nation (-100), Band of Bones and Berserkers; at truce with United Cities.
- Starts neutral (0) to a default new game.
- Police stations in Admag, Squin, Last Stand and The Great Fortress are run by a Hundred Guardian.
- The Shek Kingdom does not keep slaves.

## Shek Kingdom: how they treat you
Also: flatskin, Shek attitude to outsiders, are Shek towns safe
- Outsiders are tolerated in Shek towns for trade, on Esata's orders.
- Shek Warriors look down on "flatskins" and may harass Humans and Hivers passing through.
- Skeletons are the one other race the Shek do not despise.
- Gate guards search for Hashish, which is contraband here.
- Hundred Guardians wear heavy armour and are easy to outrun, even for new characters.
- If you are allied with The Holy Nation, Shek patrols attack unprovoked and warriors in town follow you out of the gates to attack.
- Wearing a Holy Nation uniform in Shek land gets you attacked even if you are Shek and allied.
- Building an outpost in Shek territory brings Shek Warriors demanding tribute in food.

## Shek Kingdom: allying, Bayan, Esata and Seto
Also: ally Shek, Shek reputation, Stone Golem
- Bayan, Esata's adviser in the Faction HQ in Admag, handles alliances and major bounties.
- You cannot ally with the Shek while at +50 with The Holy Nation, United Cities, Traders Guild or Slave Traders.
- Handing Bayan the Holy Lord Phoenix: c.100,000, +70 Shek Kingdom, -75 The Holy Nation.
- Handing Bayan the Bugmaster: c.100,000; +70 Shek Kingdom and -80 Holy Nation if you agree to take on the Shek's enemies, or +0 and -10 if you decline.
- Having killed either one also lets you ask Bayan for the alliance.
- Ordinary bounties handed to a Hundred Guardian give +2 each; +50 allies you, and at +80 you may speak to Esata.
- Once allied, roaming Hundred Guardians bodyguard you for 72 hours or guard your outpost for 48 hours on request.
- Harming Esata or Seto seriously damages relations; killing or kidnapping Bayan brings an assault led by Esata.

## Shek Kingdom leaders and what happens if they fall
Also: Esata the Stone Golem, Mukai the Mountain, Kamikaze, Shek Kingdom collapse
- Esata the Stone Golem sits in the Faction HQ in Admag with Seto, Bayan and four of The Five Invincibles. Bounty c.50,000 (United Cities or The Holy Nation).
- Esata killed or imprisoned: most Shek "go Kamikaze"; squads of 5 to 10 Kamikaze roam the Border Zone, Skinner's Roam, Okran's Pride and Okran's Gulf.
- Admag then falls to the Berserkers.
- Squin passes to Mukai the Mountain, or to Kral's Chosen if Mukai is dead and Flying Bull lives, or to Berserkers if both are dead.
- Hundred Guardians and Shek Warriors then swear to Flying Bull and spawn as Kral's Chosen.
- Seto killed or imprisoned: Shek patrols invade Okran's Gulf and Okran's Pride, and Holy Nation patrols start appearing in the Stenn Desert and Border Zone.

## Kral's Chosen, Berserkers and Band of Bones
Also: rogue Shek, Shek bandits, Shek outlaws
- Kral's Chosen: Shek loyal to Flying Bull, based in New Kralia; they harass and assault outsiders in the wild. Flying Bull's bounty is c.30,000 (Shek Kingdom).
- Berserkers: rogue Shek with combat skills of 40 and up, led by Ghost (c.10,000 bounty) from Berserker Village; aggressive on sight.
- Band of Bones: Shek bandits led by Tora the Fearless (c.10,000 bounty) from the Exile Camp; skills in the 20s to 30s; mostly in The Swamp and the southern United Cities; roaming squads may attack at random.
- All three are far beyond a new squad; run rather than fight.

## United Cities: overview and territory
Also: UC, the Empire, United Cities lands, United Cities towns
- Feudal slaver empire in the east and south; largest faction by land.
- Capital: Heft (Emperor Tengu). Towns: Stoat, Heng, Sho-Battai, Bark, Brink, Catun, Clownsteady, Drifter's Last; also Tengu's Vault and Farming Villages.
- Patrols roam Bast, Heng, Stormgap Coast, The Great Desert and The Hook; a typical Empire Army is 1 Samurai Sergeant, 5 to 8 Samurai, 1 to 3 Samurai Scouts and a few Samurai Conscripts.
- Allies: Traders Guild (+100), Slave Traders, Empire Peasants, Free Traders, Bounty Hunters.
- Enemies (-100): The Holy Nation, Anti-Slavers, Rebel Swordsmen, Reavers, Crab Raiders, Skin Bandits, Skeleton Bandits, Grass Pirates; at truce with the Shek Kingdom.
- Starts neutral (0) to a default new game. All races are accepted as citizens, though Hivers are mostly peasants.
- Tech Hunters and Machinists use the United Cities legal system, so crimes against them give a United Cities bounty.

## United Cities: poverty, nobles and samurai
Also: poverty is illegal, starving arrest, Noble Hunter, samurai escort
- Being visibly starving is treated as a crime.
- A Samurai Gate Sergeant tries to arrest a starving character unless you hold at least c.500.
- A patrol that finds you starving either takes pity (Honorable personality: gives 1 Bread, 1 Meatwrap, 1 Greenfruit) or tries to arrest you unless bribed with c.2,000.
- Patrolling Samurai may offer to escort you; accepting gives a bodyguard for 2 hours.
- Noble Hunters roam Stormgap Coast, The Great Desert and The Hook as a Noble Captain escorted by 6 Hooded Empire Noble Guards, and shoot travellers for sport; allies are spared.
- Attacking any noble severely harms relations.
- Being knocked out near a United Cities town risks being picked up and enslaved by Slave Traders, Manhunters or Slave Hunters.
- Gate guards search bags for Hashish, Bloodrum and Hemp.

## United Cities: raising relations and allying
Also: ally United Cities, United Cities reputation, donate to the Empire, buy alliance
- Koin, the diplomat beside Emperor Tengu in Heft, handles alliances.
- Donations to Koin: c.5,000 gives +1, c.10,000 +3, c.20,000 +10, c.50,000 +25, c.100,000 +50.
- With a level 2 or level 3 outpost, paying Koin c.10,000 gives +60.
- Handing ordinary bounties to a Police Chief gives +2 each (25 needed for alliance).
- Koin dismisses you if you are allied with The Holy Nation or Shek Kingdom, and calls guards if you are allied with the Anti-Slavers.
- Alliance perks: no bag checks, no taxes on outposts, nobles stop hunting you, samurai heal you and sometimes escort you for days.
- Allying with either United Cities or Traders Guild stops Manhunters, Slave Hunters and slave raids from targeting you.
- Alliance turns Rebel Farmers and Anti-Slavers hostile.

## United Cities leaders and what happens if they fall
Also: Emperor Tengu, Noble Circle, Lord Inaba, Lady Sanda, United Cities collapse
- Emperor Tengu: throne room in Heft, guarded by 5 Samurai Elites, 8 Samurai Captains, 2 Samurai Heavies and 5 Jonin Elites.
- Nobles and their cities: Lord Ohta and Koin (Heft), Lady Sanda (Bark), Lord Nagata (Sho-Battai), Lord Inaba (Stoat), Lord Yoshinaga (Heng), Lady Tsugi (Brink), Lord Shiro (Catun), Lady Merin (Drifter's Last).
- Tengu killed: Heft passes to Empire Peasants; Stoat and Heng become half-destroyed and fall into civil war with Rebel Swordsmen; samurai patrols thin out; Noble Hunters and Drug Bust raids stop.
- A noble killed or imprisoned: that noble's city becomes half-destroyed or changes hands (for example Stoat can go to Tech Hunters, Bark to Empire Peasants).
- Handing Tengu to The Holy Nation: +80 Holy Nation, -75 United Cities.
- Being caught stealing inside a Noble House brings a Noble's Wrath attack on your outpost.

## Traders Guild
Also: Trader's Guild, merchant guild, buy Traders Guild alliance
- Trading and slaving organisation behind the United Cities economy; HQ at Trader's Edge, leader Longen.
- Always starts neutral, except the Wandering Trader game start (+75).
- Alliance is sold by Yamdu at Trader's Edge: c.100,000, or c.75,000 if you own an outpost. Result: +75 Traders Guild, +75 Slave Traders, -30 Anti-Slavers.
- Pro-slavery answers to Longen or Lady Kana raise relations; anti-slavery answers lower them.
- Killing or imprisoning Longen or Lady Kana seriously damages relations and blocks the Pacifier.
- Longen killed: Trader's Edge falls into ruin and Traders Guild caravans and Starving Vagrants stop spawning.
- The guild will not buy Hashish.

## Slave Traders
Also: slavers, Slavemonger, Slaver Boss, slaver faction
- The slave-owning branch tied to the Traders Guild; run Slave Shops, slave farms and camps.
- Locations: Eyesocket, Slave Farm, Slave Farm South, Slave Markets, South Stone Camp, Stone Camp; Slave Shops in Bark, Heft, Sho-Battai, Port North, Port South and others.
- Not hostile unless your relation is hostile, but they pick up any unconscious non-allied character, heal them and take them to a slave site.
- Squads of 1 to 8 Slavemongers also roam the Border Zone.
- Hostile to Anti-Slavers, Mongrel, Rebel Farmers, escaped slaves and ex-slaves.
- Buying slaves from a Slaver Boss raises relations by 1 each; the Koin and Yamdu payments above also raise Slave Traders relations.
- Slave Master Grande killed or imprisoned: Eyesocket falls to ruins.

## Manhunters and Slave Hunters
Also: escaped slave hunters, kidnappers in the desert
- Both roam United Cities territory catching escaped slaves and selling them to Slave Traders.
- They also attack travellers they judge weaker than themselves and sell them as "escaped slaves".
- Manhunters leave you alone if you are allied with United Cities or Traders Guild, if police are nearby, inside an NPC town, or if your squad outnumbers theirs.
- Hired mercenaries nearby deter Manhunters; Slave Hunters ignore mercenaries and attack anyway.
- Slave Hunters are the weaker, worse-equipped version.
- Both pick up unconscious characters and haul them to a slave location.
- A real escaped slave is chased regardless of alliances.

## Empire Peasants, Vagrants and Starving Vagrants
Also: peasants, desert beggars, Vagrant Camp
- Empire Peasants: poor, largely Hiver citizens of the United Cities living in shacks and camps; allied with the United Cities and hostile to The Holy Nation.
- Vagrants: a loose category of destitute characters; some sit in bars, many rob travellers in The Great Desert and Heng.
- Starving Vagrants are the United Cities' counterpart of Starving Bandits, slightly better armed, with sharper weapons.
- Manhunters and Slave Hunters constantly capture them.
- A captured Starving Vagrant sells for c.400 at a Slave Shop.

## Rebel Farmers and Rebel Swordsmen
Also: peasant rebels, farmer rebellion, rebels
- Rebel Farmers: evicted United Cities peasants turned bandits across Empire lands; HQ Fort Simion, leader Boss Simion (c.20,000 bounty).
- They attack travellers on the roads, not only nobles.
- Joining them through Boss Simion: +75 Rebel Farmers, +75 Rebel Swordsmen, +10 Anti-Slavers, -25 United Cities, -25 Slave Traders.
- Rebels in the Barracks at Fort Simion usually do not turn hostile when you steal from their containers, which makes safe Thievery practice.
- Rebel Swordsmen only exist after some United Cities nobles are killed or kidnapped; they pay bounties like the United Cities does.
- Captured Rebel Farmers sell for c.400 at a Slave Shop.

## Anti-Slavers
Also: anti-slavery faction, abolitionists, free the slaves
- Elite martial-artist faction fighting slavery; base at Spring, in the mountains of Stobe's Gamble. Leader: the Skeleton Tinfist (c.100,000 bounty).
- Hostile to United Cities, Traders Guild, Slave Traders, Manhunters, Slave Hunters and The Holy Nation.
- Join by talking to Grey at Spring: +75 Anti-Slavers, -30 United Cities, Traders Guild and Slave Traders.
- Grey accepts a character with the escaped-slave or ex-slave status as a refugee, and clears that status when spoken to.
- Having killed or captured Longen and then talking to Tinfist gives +75 without the -30 penalties.
- Grey attacks on sight if you are allied with The Holy Nation, United Cities, Traders Guild or Slave Traders.
- Allying locks most Police Chief dialogue and makes Holy Nation Inquisitors attack when spoken to.
- Tinfist killed or imprisoned: slavers expand and Slave Traders patrols appear in more zones.

## Tech Hunters and Machinists
Also: tech hunter faction, Drifter, Machinist
- Tech Hunters: neutral adventurers who control World's End, Black Scratch, Flats Lagoon, Mourn and the Waystations (Border Zone, Grey Desert, Stenn Desert, The Hook).
- They run shops inside United Cities, Shek Kingdom, Mongrel and Anti-Slaver towns.
- Their bounty system is tied to the United Cities: a crime against Tech Hunters gives a United Cities bounty.
- No goods are contraband in their towns.
- Being caught stealing lowers relations.
- Tech Hunter squads can be hired as mercenaries in bars.
- Machinists: scholars at the University in World's End; knocking out their leader Finch gives -100.
- The Border Zone Waystation, south-east in the zone, has a bar with recruits and iron and copper nearby.

## Shinobi Thieves
Also: Thieves Guild membership, join thieves, Thief Boss, thieves faction, 10,000 cats to join
- Thieves, fences and smugglers with a tower (Thieves Guild) in Admag, Catun, Clownsteady, Heft, Heng, Mongrel, Sho-Battai and The Hub.
- Join by paying c.10,000 to the Thief Boss standing outside a tower; no quest needed.
- Membership gives access to the tower's beds, Assassination Dummies, Locksmith Training Boxes, the Shinobi Trader and the Guild Plastic Surgeon.
- Members pay half the list price at the Shinobi Trader: Small Thieves Backpack c.1,500 (list c.3,000), Thieves Backpack c.2,500 (list c.5,000).
- Thieves and Ninja Guards then fight for you near their tower, and Wandering Assassins heal you.
- They buy contraband (Hashish, Bloodrum) that no other trader in United Cities towns will touch.
- Non-members using the tower's training gear should stay in stealth and stop when seen, or the thieves turn hostile.
- Thief Fences in ordinary bars trade with anyone.
- Known bug: if you reach allied relations without paying, the join dialogue may complete without charging and without making you a member.

## Mercenary Guild
Also: mercenaries, hire bodyguards, hiring help, Mercenary Captain, hired fighters, protection for a weak squad
- Mercenary squads (a Mercenary Captain with Mercenaries and Mercenary Heavies, about four in a group) sit in bars and travel between Holy Nation and United Cities lands.
- Talk to the captain to hire; renew by talking again. They follow and fight whatever attacks you but are not yours to control.
- The wiki disagrees on price: its Mercenary Guild page says c.2,000 per day as bodyguards and c.1,000 per day as outpost guards; its Events page says c.1,000 per day (up to two days) as bodyguards and c.2,000 per two days (up to eight) for an outpost.
- Competent fighters with very poor gear, far stronger than a new character: useful for one dangerous trip, not as a way of life at beginner income.
- Tech Hunters, Bar Thugs and Cannibal Hunters (c.3,000 per day) can be hired in bars the same way.
- United Cities samurai patrols sometimes offer a free 2-hour escort.
- The clicks: "Hire mercenary bodyguards" in How-to.

## Nomads
Also: nomad faction, animal traders, nomad herders
- Neutral human herders who wander with pack animals, goats and a Bonedog.
- Found in the Border Zone, Stenn Desert, Shem, Okran's Gulf and Dreg, and in the north-east (The Great Desert, Heng, Grey Desert and others).
- The Nomad Animal Trader sells animals.
- Their camps are free to use and usually have shelter, a campfire and a Camp Bed.

## Western Hive
Also: Hive faction, hiver faction, hiveless
- Insect-like Hive people living in Hive Villages along the river in Vain, west of the Border Zone, and in Dreg.
- Neutral to the player and neutral with The Holy Nation.
- Villagers refuse to deal with "hiveless" Hive player characters; Hive caravans trade with them normally.
- Hive Villages sell Hashish.
- If the Hive Queen is killed, hive bandits and lost hivers start roaming many zones.
- Vain itself is dangerous for a new squad because of Beak Things.

## Flotsam Ninjas
Also: Flotsam, ninja rebels, women rebels
- Rebels, mostly women who fled The Holy Nation and escaped Rebirth slaves; hidden Flotsam Village in the Hidden Forest, near the Cannibal Plains.
- Leader: Moll (c.50,000 bounty from The Holy Nation).
- Joining by pledging to Moll costs -75 with The Holy Nation.
- Agreeing with Moll's rant about religion gives +2 each time and can be repeated up to alliance.
- Moll accepts the Phoenix (+75 Flotsam Ninjas), Seta and Valtena (+40 each) as prisoners; the wiki's bounty table is unclear on whether she pays cash for them.
- Moll killed or imprisoned: Flotsam Village is taken by Cannibals.
- Several unique recruits wait in Flotsam Village: Digna, Pia, Reva, Knife.

## Mongrel (faction)
Also: Mongrel relations, Mongrel enemies, Mongrel leader
- Independent town in the middle of the Fog Islands, ringed by hostile Fogmen.
- Enemy of The Holy Nation and of Slave Traders (-100 each way).
- Houses in Mongrel are private; entering them is trespassing.
- Harming its leader Arc seriously damages relations.
- Home of the unique recruits Beep, Shryke, Crumblejon and Infinite Wingwang.
- Reaching it means crossing the Fog Islands, where Fogmen attack anyone.
- Shops, houses and routes: "Mongrel (town)" in Towns.

## Deadcat and Cannibal Hunters
Also: Deadcat faction, cannibal hunter faction
- Deadcat: a small refugee faction of fishing villages on the Northern Coast and in Dreg; neutral.
- Their Fishing Village bar is the most likely place to find Hobbs, plus Horse, Soman and Green Finger.
- Cannibal Hunters: based at The Bastion, hostile to Cannibals; they may heal wounded strangers and can be hired for c.3,000 per day.
- The surrounding plains are overrun by Cannibals.

## The Swamp gangs: Swampers and Hounds
Also: swamp gangs, Blackshifters, Grayflayers, Stone Rats, Twinblades, Big Grim
- The Swamp is run by five gangs: Hounds (strongest, leader Big Grim), Blackshifters, Grayflayers, Stone Rats, Twinblades; the unaligned locals are Swampers. Main town: Shark.
- Nothing is contraband there; Hashish and Hemp are grown and sold cheaply.
- To deal with the Hounds, pay Ears in The Dancing Skeleton bar in Shark c.2,000, then speak to Big Grim.
- Red Sabres (Red Sabre Boss, c.10,000 bounty) and Swamp Ninjas (Shade, c.20,000) are hostile bandit gangs that also fight each other.
- Blood Spiders make The Swamp deadly for a new squad.
- Free unique recruits in Shark and Mud Town: Miu, Doctor Chung, Hamut.

## Factions that are always hostile
Also: attack on sight, cannot be befriended, monsters
- Cannibals (Cannibal Plains, Darkfinger and the north): carry unconscious people to their camps and eat them.
- Fogmen (Fog Islands): start at -100; tie captives to poles and eat them alive.
- Southern Hive (Royal Valley, Greyshelf): hostile whatever you do.
- Bloodraiders (The Eye, Grey Desert, Spine Canyon): start at -100.
- Grass Pirates (The Eye, Stormgap Coast) and Yabuta Outlaws: always hostile.
- Skeleton Legion (Sonorous Dark, Ashlands) and Black Desert Ninjas (Deadlands): always hostile.
- Berserkers and Swamp Ninjas attack any person they see.
- Sand Ninjas (Spine Canyon, The Great Desert; leader Sand Ninja Oni, c.20,000 bounty) attack passers-by they judge weak.

## South-east gangs: Reavers, Crab Raiders, Skin Bandits, Skeleton Bandits
Also: south-east bandit factions, Reaver faction, Crab Raider faction
- Four warring factions in the far south-east; allying with one makes enemies of the others (typically -75).
- Reavers (leader Valamon, HQ Ark): slavers who capture travellers and force them to fight.
- Crab Raiders (Crab Queen, Crab Town): Scorchlanders who worship crabs; winning a Crab Tournament raises relations.
- Skin Bandits (Savant, Skinhouse HQ): Skeletons who skin humans in Peeler Machines; neutral to Hiver, Skeleton and Shek characters; only a Skeleton can ally with them.
- Skeleton Bandits (Elder, Iron HQ): humans in Skeleton masks; hostile to organic races; alliance through Elder.
- All far from the starting regions and far too strong for a new squad.

## Skeletons of Black Desert City
Also: Skeleton faction, Black Desert City faction
- A neutral faction of ancient Skeletons living in Black Desert City in the Deadlands.
- Acid rain there kills most organic characters on the way.
- No Pacifier exists for this faction.
- The free Skeleton recruit Sadneil sits in the Skeleton Bar there.

## Bounty Hunters and the United Heroes League
Also: City Hero, vigilantes, UHL
- Bounty Hunters: mostly United Cities citizens who attack characters with a large enough bounty and may attack anyone they see committing a crime.
- United Heroes League: Greenlander "city heroes" who assault non-human characters around United Cities towns; HQ Fort Mirage.
- City Heroes are classed as bandits: you may attack them inside towns and the guards take your side.
- The Shek Kingdom and Anti-Slavers are hostile to the League.
